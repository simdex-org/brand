01 · The brand

An open workshop, not a showroom.

Simdex is an open-source organization and community where developers, modders and tinkerers build tools that make other people's lives easier. The identity has to feel like a shared workbench: tidy, useful and open to anyone who walks in.

Built in the open.
Build, share, improve.

Purpose. Create, develop and distribute technology projects without restrictions, so anyone can use them, study them and make them better.

Promise. Every Simdex project solves a real problem, can be read from start to finish and welcomes your pull request.

Audience. Developers, modders, indie game makers, power users and people who are learning to code.

Three pillars

simdex/games

Games and modding

Indie games, mods, graphics libraries and open assets built with Godot, Blender or Bevy. The creative side: mechanics, mod translations, simulations and community expansions.

simdex/cli

Tools and CLIs

Automation, command-line programs, scripts and optimizers. The toolbox: lightweight, fast and with the code in plain sight.

simdex/ext

Extensions and plugins

Browser extensions, plugins for IDEs like VS Code and add-ons for game engines. The connective tissue: making the tools people already use more powerful.

Values

Open collaboration

Anyone can propose an idea, open a pull request or report a bug. Simdex doesn't belong to any single person.

Practical value

We prefer tools that work today over theoretical perfection. However small, if it solves something real, it counts.

Accessibility and learning

Simdex is also mentorship: a place to gain experience on real projects of any size and stack.

02 · Logo

Two hooks and a node.

The mark is an S split into two pieces that lock together by rotating 180°, with a mint node at the center. The pieces stand for the person who proposes and the person who contributes; the node is where they connect: the commit, the cursor, the spark. The wordmark is Sora Bold converted to outlines, and it repeats the node as the dot of the i.

Mark
Wordmark · Sora Bold outlines

Pieces that fit

Two identical modules, one rotated. Nobody builds alone: the project and the community complete each other.

The node

A connection point: it reads as a pixel, a blinking cursor or the socket of a plugin. It is the only accent color in the mark.

Code and play

The hooks echo brackets and board-game pieces. The rotational symmetry speaks of reciprocity: what you take, you give back.

03 · Construction and sizing

One module: x.

The whole mark is measured with a single unit: x, the stroke width, which equals the diameter of the node. That unit rebuilds the symbol, sets the clear space and fixes the minimum sizes.

x
Stroke width and node diameter. The unit for the whole system.
½x
Gap between each hook and the node. It never closes: the gap is what makes the connection readable.
0.85x
Centerline radius of the curves. Both curves are identical and turn around symmetric centers.
180°
The lower piece is the upper piece rotated half a turn around the node. Each straight end stops exactly at the outer edge of the opposite curve.
4.7 × 4.4x
Overall box of the mark. In the horizontal logo, the x-height of the wordmark is 2.8x.

Clear space

Keep at least 1x of empty space around the logo (the diameter of the node). No text, border or image may enter that area. When there's room, use 2x.

Minimum sizes

Mark

At least 24 px on screen and 8 mm in print. At 16 px, only as a favicon, using the app icon.

Horizontal

At least 96 px wide on screen and 25 mm in print. Below that, use the mark.

04 · Color versions

Prussian first.

The primary version is Prussian blue with a dark mint node (#2E9C7E) on a light background. On Prussian blue it inverts and the node goes back to light mint. On lavender the node turns paper white; on cyan it keeps the mint. The one-color versions are for engraving, stamps, single-ink printing and photographic backgrounds.

05 · Misuse

What we never do.

The logo is a file, not a starting point. Always use the official SVGs, unmodified. If a format doesn't allow any approved version, open an issue in the brand repository.

06 · Color palette

Cyanotype.

The palette comes from cyanotype, the process used to copy blueprints: documents made to be reproduced and shared, just like open source. Prussian blue carries the brand, cyan adds light, mint is the node that connects everything and lavender softens surfaces. The neutrals carry a blue tint so nothing looks gray by default.

07 · Typography

Character, reading and code.

Three free families from Google Fonts, fitting for an open-source brand: anyone can download and use them with no license fees. The reading face was designed for people with low vision, a direct nod to our accessibility value.

Headlines · Display Aa Sora ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz 0123456789

Weights 600–800, line height 0.95–1.1, tracking −2%. Only for headlines, large figures and campaign pieces. It's the same family as the wordmark, so headlines and brand speak with one voice.

Text · Reading Atkinson Hyperlegible Aa Bb Cc Il1 O0 rn m · Regular 400 · Bold 700

Paragraphs, interfaces and documentation. 16–18 px, line height 1.6, at most 65 characters per line.

Code · Utility JetBrains Mono $ simdex init --template godot

Commands, repository names, labels and technical data. For labels: uppercase with +8% tracking.

08 · Graphic elements

The Circuit pattern.

The pattern grows out of the mark's curves: tiles with two quarter circles that, rotated at random, form continuous paths, like circuit traces or routes on a game map. Mint nodes mark connection points. Use it for backgrounds, covers and merch, never behind small text.

On lavender

Iconography

Heavy-stroke icons on a 48 px grid, with square ends and sharp corners, just like the logo. One color; cyan is reserved for a single point of emphasis.

Pillar sub-brands

/games

Games, mods and assets. Support color: lavender.

/cli

Command-line tools and automation. Support color: cyan.

/ext

Extensions and plugins. Support color: Prussian blue.

The pillar name is set in JetBrains Mono with a slash, like a repository path. Gap from the logo: ½x. The slash name matches the wordmark's x-height.

09 · Voice and tone

We talk like someone handing you the screwdriver.

We write like an experienced teammate: clear, direct and never showing off. We explain the why, celebrate the first contribution as much as the hundredth, and never make anyone feel bad for not knowing something.

Direct, not curt

We get to the point, warmly. A clear instruction is a form of respect.

Technical, not cryptic

We use the right term and explain it the first time it appears.

Friendly, not childish

Humor fits. Sarcasm doesn't.

Enthusiastic, not hyped

We say what the tool does, without promising revolutions.

Do
  • Is this your first PR? Great. Tag it first-contribution and we'll help you review it.
  • simdex/cli v2.0 starts in 40 ms. The details are in the changelog.
  • That bug is on us. There's an open issue and we're fixing it.
Don't
  • Contributions that do not strictly comply with the guidelines will be rejected.
  • The ultimate tool that will change the way you code forever.
  • User error. Read the docs.
